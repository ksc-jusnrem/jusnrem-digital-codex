Doctrine Register

A doctrine does not belong to the chapter that first states it. It is addressed independently, so that works cite the Register rather than restating it — which is what the JUS.automica Charter already requires: foundational doctrines are stated in full only in Volume I, and applied and cross-referenced thereafter.

What this register does and does not claim

Every entry carries its status, and no entry is presented as settled doctrine. The programme's own records show no source yet cleared for citation and no chapter yet at treatise grade, so entries stand as working or as located candidates. That is the honest position, and stating it is the point of a register.

Names in the first table are the Author's, taken from a ratified instrument. Entries in the second are analytical constructs located in the published text of Code, Law and Capital and cited to the paragraph that states them. Most are not given canonical doctrinal names in the work. No name has been invented here. Naming them is an authorial act.
