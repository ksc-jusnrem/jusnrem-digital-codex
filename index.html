02 Doctrine Register

A doctrine is cited across works, so it needs an address of its own.

Doctrines do not belong to the chapter that first states them. The Register gives each one a permanent record — canonical name, authorial definition, provenance and first development, jurisprudential foundation, legal elements, the applicable test, counterarguments, related doctrines, institutional examples — and a status of exploratory, developed, settled or revised. Works then cite the Register rather than restating it.

03 How the Codex is governed

Claims must remain smaller than the evidence.

Status is stated, not implied
Every proposition carries its research status. Publication does not convert a candidate proposition into established law or an institutional deployment.
Sources are separated by rank
Constitutions, enacted statutes, notified instruments and binding judgments are distinguished from policy, consultation history, guidance and empirical assertion. Where the authenticated instrument is not held, the proposition is qualified rather than upgraded by inference.
Nothing is silently overwritten
Substantive updates carry a date, a version and an explanation of what changed and why. Earlier formulations remain part of the record.
Text is addressed, not paginated
Each work, chapter, section, paragraph and doctrine receives an identifier intended to survive revision, so the digital edition can be cited precisely.

The permanent citation scheme is still being fixed. Paragraph numbering in released chapters is provisional and should not yet be relied on for fixed pinpoint citation.

These publications are independent evidence-controlled analysis. They are not legal advice to any person, a regulatory approval, an offer of investment, a prediction of market performance, or a substitute for checking the law applicable to a particular activity at the relevant time. Nothing here represents governmental authority.

04 Living updates

A treatise that cannot record change becomes a historical document.

Legislative and regulatory developments, judgments and administrative decisions, standards and technical change, and jurisdictional comparisons will be recorded against the propositions they affect — each dated, with its consequence for the earlier text stated rather than absorbed in silence.
