26.1 Construction, Not Assumed Reform

26.1.1 Why Pakistan is completing an incomplete order rather than repairing a mature one

1
The Virtual Assets Act 2026 created the Pakistan Virtual Assets Regulatory Authority, defined regulated functions and conferred rulemaking, licensing, supervisory and enforcement powers.1 The notified Regulations add an operative licensing and conduct baseline.2 Yet Pakistan did not inherit a mature, fully licensed market with settled custody, tax, insolvency and inter-regulator practice. The proper verb is construction: bringing law, institutions, records, staffing, systems and remedies into a coherent order.

26.1.2 The minimum elements of an operational legal system

2
An operational system needs more than an Act. It needs commenced instruments; appointed and delegated decision-makers; published forms and service standards; an authoritative register; licensed firms; supervisory data; enforceable customer-asset rules; investigations; reasons; complaints; appeals; judicial review; and resources. 26.2 The Present Position of Citizens, Holders and Incumbent Providers

26.2.1 Current exposure, uncertainty and evidential burdens

5
Citizens may hold assets acquired through foreign platforms, peer-to-peer transactions, gifts, mining or other routes. Their immediate problems are evidential: identity of counterparties, acquisition cost, source of funds, wallet control, tax valuation and availability of records after platform failure. Holding, using, marketing, transmitting and operating a business are distinct. The law should not turn uncertainty in one category into a presumption about all the others.

26.2.2 Immediate need for authoritative public transition guidance

6
PVARA should publish one consolidated transition notice stating the commencement dates, activities requiring permission, treatment of existing complete applications, marketing restrictions, customer-asset duties, forms, fees, decision timelines, complaints route and consequences of refusal. SBP, SECP, FBR, FMU and relevant enforcement bodies should join or formally cross-reference the notice where their powers are affected. Frequently asked questions may explain the law but cannot enlarge it.

26.2.3 The authoritative transition notice and proof of continuity

7
Section 70 gives an existing provider a six-month bridge only if it submits a complete licence application within the statutory period and complies with interim directions and core obligations. Section 74 saves specified pre-Act matters, while section 73 permits removal of difficulties for six months but not an order inconsistent with the Act. These provisions should not be collapsed into a general amnesty.32 The exact terminal day should be confirmed by reasoned legal determination and officially published; this treatise does not manufacture certainty between 4 and 5 September 2026. The transition notice should state:
8
the class of provider capable of relying on section 70 and the evidence of existing activity;
9
what makes an application “complete”, how deficiencies are notified and whether a cured filing retains its original date;
10
the status of NOC holders, non-holders and applicants at each stage;
11
the obligations that apply during the bridge, including safeguarding, AML/CFT, disclosure, incident reporting and cooperation;
12
the consequences of rejection, withdrawal, incompleteness or expiry; and
13
customer-protection, transfer and wind-down requirements where authority to continue ends.
14
The notice must distinguish law from assistance. A checklist cannot extend the statutory period, and an NOC or pending application cannot be described as a licence. If the enacted transition cannot safely be completed, Parliament—not guidance—must alter it.

26.3 Legal Validity and the Authoritative Instruments Register

26.3.1 Commencement, appointments, delegations and review routes

15
For every power, the public record should show the enabling provision, commencement, office-holder, delegation, territorial scope, procedure and route of review. Delegations should identify the function and conditions rather than use generic internal authority. A decision made by the wrong person is not cured by its substantive attractiveness.

26.3.2 One authenticated and versioned public legal record

16
PVARA should maintain an authenticated, searchable register containing the Act, current Rules and Regulations, commencement instruments, delegations intended for publication, forms, fees, binding directions, general guidance, licence conditions of general application, amendments and superseded versions. Each item should carry source, date, legal status and effective period. A website upload without provenance should not be treated as law.

26.3.3 Authentication of appointments, delegations and decision authority

17
Before a body exercises coercive or determinative power, the public record should identify the lawful office-holder, appointment instrument, commencement date, term, vacancy status, delegation and any recusal. The Authority should publish a consolidated governance page linking the Board constitution, chairperson and member notifications, authorised committees, delegations under section 10, and the officers empowered to issue notices, decide applications, inspect, freeze, sanction or defend proceedings.33 Sensitive personal material need not be exposed. Legal authority must be.
18
Delegation instruments should identify the source power, delegate, subject, conditions, duration and retained powers, and preserve non-delegable functions. Proof requires a public delegation register cross-checked against protected signature and access controls.

26.3.4 The operative, historical and superseded instruments record

19
The operative regime cannot be reconstructed from press releases, unversioned downloads and draft handbooks. PVARA should maintain a single public register containing the Act, saved instruments, Federal Rules, PVARA Regulations, Gazette references, commencement and repeal data, binding standards, non-binding guidance, general directions, consultation drafts, feedback statements, forms and superseded versions. Every item should show its legal basis, status, issuer, approval date, effective date and checksum. Drafts should carry a permanent watermark and cease to appear beside operative instruments without a status distinction.
20
Publication and version control determine notice, reliance, equality of treatment and the evidence available to a court. The Pakistan Virtual Asset Services Regulations, 2026 and Pakistan Virtual Asset Activity-Specific Regulations, 2026 are notified. PVARA should publish and preserve the authoritative text, notification particulars, effective dates, amendments, superseded consultation versions and a current consolidated version so that their legal status and temporal application cannot be misunderstood.35

26.4 A Lawful Transition and Continuity Framework

26.4.1 Existing holders, own-account P2P users and incumbent providers

21
Section 70 of the Act gives an existing VASP six months from commencement to apply or cease; a person who files a complete application within time may continue the existing services pending determination, subject to interim directions and core obligations concerning customer assets and financial integrity.3 Regulation 5A gives operational detail, including prior approval for marketing during transition.4 That continuity is provider-, service-, time- and application-specific. It does not automatically cover a new service or an incomplete application.

26.4.2 No implied amnesty; any grace, safe harbour or regularisation must be express, bounded and legally valid

22
Neither transition language nor regulatory silence creates a general amnesty for earlier contraventions, untaxed gains or unexplained funds. Conversely, the absence of a general amnesty does not make every holder or own-account peer-to-peer user a VASP. Any regularisation route must state eligible persons, covered periods, disclosure, tax and AML treatment, exclusions, decision-maker, confidentiality, evidential effect and relation to criminal proceedings.

26.4.3 Instrument-by-instrument continuity determination

23
Section 74 should be applied instrument by instrument. For each Ordinance-era NOC regulation, direction, application, order and institutional act, the responsible authority should record: the pre-Act source; whether the subject survives under the Act; whether the content is inconsistent; the new statutory home; any adaptation required; and the date on which it will be replaced. Where the Act requires consultation, Gazette publication or a different decision-maker, savings cannot be used to evade that requirement.
24
The determination should preserve customer protection and procedural continuity without declaring an NOC equivalent to a section 19 licence. Section 73 may remove a genuine implementation difficulty within its limited life, but it should not be used to add a regulated class, create a levy, enlarge a penalty, change appellate jurisdiction or rewrite the transition. Those are questions of legislative choice.

26.5 Primary Legislative Completion

26.5.1 Perimeter, issuers, customer assets and insolvency

25
The Act and Regulations should be tested for issues that delegated law cannot safely settle alone. Primary law should state the boundary with deposits, securities, derivatives and payment instruments; legal consequences of issuer redemption promises; recognition and transfer of digital assets; customer beneficial interests; segregation; reuse; shortfalls; priority; insolvency exclusion; and return or transfer. Criminal liability and compulsory deprivation also require clear legislative authority.

26.5.2 Enforcement, appeals, tax and regulator interfaces

26
Primary law should allocate investigation, administrative penalties, freezing, confiscation, compounding, appeals and judicial review with procedural safeguards. It should reconcile PVARA, SBP, SECP, FBR, FMU, FIA and provincial functions without implied transfer. Tax charge, valuation, withholding, information and regularisation cannot rest on regulatory guidance alone. A statutory interface mechanism should resolve classification without erasing each body's legal competence.

26.5.3 Clarifying the perimeter and regulator interfaces

27
The Act should preserve its functional service definition but add an express substance-first rule: characterisation under the VAA does not displace the law governing currency, deposits, payment systems, securities, derivatives, collective investment, companies, secured transactions, tax or underlying property. PVARA should classify the virtual-asset service; SBP, SECP and other competent authorities should decide questions Parliament has assigned to them. A joint determination mechanism should resolve mixed cases without allowing silence by one body to become approval by another.36
28
The amendment should authorise a public perimeter register recording anonymised or publishable determinations, the facts treated as decisive, participating agencies and resulting permissions. It should also state the Pakistan nexus for remote and cross-border offerings: targeted solicitation, local distribution, local-currency access, local establishment, material local customer base or other objectively specified connection. A reverse-solicitation exception, if adopted, must be narrow, evidenced and unavailable where the provider cultivates the Pakistan market.

26.5.4 Completing the issuer lifecycle

29
Sections 18–23, 30–33, 42–43 and Schedule I do not yet supply an entirely clear route for the person issuing on its own behalf. Parliament should distinguish a principal issuer from a professional third-party issuance service provider and prohibit public offering, admission or targeted distribution without the appropriate approval. The statute should confer express power to require a white paper or equivalent disclosure, identify the legally responsible issuer, control marketing, impose continuing disclosure, require redemption or reserve arrangements where promised, and suspend or withdraw an offering.37
30
The primary text should state the legal consequences of material misstatement, omission, unauthorised offering and failure to honour redemption. It should identify who may claim, the limitation framework, available compensation or rescission, and the relationship with securities and company law. It should not validate an “asset-referenced”, “stable”, “Shariah-compliant” or “real-world-asset” claim by name alone. Where a token represents title, a security interest or a beneficial interest, the underlying law must recognise and perfect that right.

26.5.5 Making customer-asset protection portable across insolvency

31
Section 24 already establishes segregation, fiduciary responsibility and exclusion of customer assets from the VASP’s estate. The amendment should make the protective consequences executable across custody chains and insolvency forums. It should address sub-custody, omnibus holdings, shortfalls, reconciliation, tracing, permitted lien or set-off, customer consent, return in specie, valuation where return is impossible, and recognition by liquidators and courts.38
32
The legislation should authorise a Pakistani-law statutory trust or equivalent proprietary mechanism so ownership, control, priority and return can be proved when a VASP fails. Reuse or encumbrance should require authority, informed consent, activity-specific permission and clear loss allocation.

26.5.6 Repairing AML/CFT allocation and the issuer gap

33
The VAA should identify PVARA’s AML/CFT supervisory role in terms aligned with the Anti-Money Laundering Act 2010 and the lawful designation architecture, without compromising the Financial Monitoring Unit’s protected functions. If the relevant Schedule IV allocation or notification has not been validly made and published, it should be made through the instrument required by the AMLA. The VAA’s reference to “FMLI” should be corrected to the legally intended institution.39
34
The reporting-entity position of principal issuers and other covered participants should be stated expressly rather than inferred from a VASP definition designed around services to third parties. Parliament should confer power for risk-based treatment of self-hosted addresses, unhosted transfers, privacy-enhancing features and cross-border originator/beneficiary information, while preserving legality, proportionality, data minimisation, confidentiality and effective challenge.

26.5.7 Correcting enforcement, penalties and appellate architecture

35
The amendment should implement the Chapter 16 enforcement chain: a single notice architecture; particularised grounds; access to adverse material subject to lawful protection; opportunity to respond except for justified urgent interim action; time-bounded freezes and restrictions; prompt confirmation; reasons; internal reconsideration where appropriate; and effective independent review. Sections 57, 59, 60 and 62–65 require coordinated redrafting so that prohibited conduct, maximum exposure, review, condonation, stay, appellate route and the relationship with constitutional jurisdiction are intelligible.40
36
The law should distinguish administrative, civil and criminal routes; prohibit double punishment for the same wrong; and state how settlement affects other proceedings. Digital-evidence and key-custody powers require defined thresholds, records, proportionality, return and remedy. Penalties should not import an unlimited maximum from “any other law”.

26.5.8 Correcting public-finance, tax and textual defects

37
Sections 13–15 and the annual-report provision should be aligned with the Constitution, the Public Finance Management Act 2019, federal accounting, audit and appropriation requirements. The amendment should clarify the legal status, custody, permitted application and reporting of the PVARA Fund; retain Auditor-General and parliamentary visibility; and prevent fees or monetary penalties from becoming an off-budget operational incentive.41
38
Section 66’s reference to the “Income Tax Act” should be corrected to the Income Tax Ordinance 2001. The correction must not be mistaken for creation of a tax charge. Any digital-asset charging, withholding, reporting, valuation or loss rule belongs in the relevant fiscal legislation enacted under the constitutional allocation of taxing power.42 The Act’s numbering and cross-references should be technically corrected, including the annual-report sequence. Section 70 should be amended only if the transition truly cannot be completed lawfully; section 74 should remain a savings provision, not a permanent substitute for Act-era instruments.
39
Finally, Parliament should decide—not leave to a handbook—whether and how the Authority’s Shariah advisory function relates to a token or product presented as Shariah-compliant. The statute need not make the regulator a religious certifier. It should prevent misleading claims, identify any recognised approval process and preserve the powers of financial-sector regulators whose laws govern the underlying product.

Consultation should publish the legal power, problem, proposed text, impact and response. They should be modular enough to update a custody control without reopening unrelated mining or advisory rules, but integrated enough to avoid contradictory definitions and duplicated sanctions. It should identify the source and maximum consequence of non-compliance rather than create a free-standing punitive code. Where a handbook contains a mandatory rule, that rule should migrate into the Regulation or a standard validly incorporated under it.

26.6.5 Standards and technical specifications

64
Standards are appropriate for matters that require precision and controlled revision: Travel Rule message fields and interoperability; customer-asset reconciliation files; wallet-control evidence; cybersecurity baselines; incident categories; application data dictionaries; regulatory returns; proof-of-reserve methodology if used; algorithmic-token stress scenarios; and audit-log retention. Each binding standard should identify the enabling Regulation, version, effective date, change log, conformance test and exception process.
65
Technical requirements should be outcome-linked. “Use industry-standard security” is not testable. A useful standard states the protected outcome, minimum control, evidence required, test frequency, severity classification and time to remediate. Where several implementations can achieve the result, the standard should avoid locking the market into a named commercial vendor or a cryptographic method that cannot be revised safely.

26.6.6 Guidance, supervisory statements and forms

66
Guidance should explain how PVARA interprets and administers the law, give worked examples and disclose supervisory expectations. It should say that it is non-binding, identify the provisions explained and state that a materially different lawful method can be demonstrated. A regulated person should not be sanctioned merely for departing from guidance where it complies with the binding rule and achieves the required outcome.
67
Forms and portals should collect only information lawfully required. Each field should map to an assessment or reporting purpose; sensitive information should have a retention, access and deletion rule. Portal validation should distinguish a technically accepted upload from a legally complete application. Automated risk scores may support triage but should not become undisclosed decision criteria.

26.6.7 Consultation, amendment and commencement controls

68
For future amendments and remaining regulatory modules, PVARA should publish a disposition table showing material comments, accepted or rejected recommendation, reason, and textual consequence. It should perform an authority review, constitutional-rights review, competition and small-provider impact assessment, privacy and cybersecurity assessment, implementation-cost estimate and regulator-capacity test. If a rule cannot be supervised at commencement, that is a design defect, not a reason to pretend compliance can be inferred.
69
Future amendments and remaining modules should use staged commencement. Governance, fitness, customer-asset, AML/CFT, disclosure, incident-notification and records duties should apply first or remain continuously applicable. Requirements dependent on new schemas or systems may commence after published conformance testing. Grandfathering should protect orderly transition, not exempt inherited business indefinitely. PVARA should publish the signed final instruments, Gazette evidence where applicable, a clean text and a redline against the consultation draft.44

26.7 Licensing and Market Entry

26.7.1 Entity status, activity permission and continuing fitness

70
Licensing should separate incorporation approval from permission to operate. Under the Services Regulations, an NOC is preliminary approval for incorporation and is not a licence.5 Final permission should identify each activity, customer class, product limit, territory and condition. Continuing fitness includes controllers, management, capital, governance, custody, technology, complaints and truthful reporting.

26.7.2 Foreign providers, local presence, exit and failure

71
Foreign providers serving Pakistan require a clear connecting test, local accountable presence where justified, access to records and enforceable customer redress. Equivalence should be statutory and outcome-based, not inferred from a foreign licence. Every licence should include recovery, wind-down and transfer plans, pre-positioned records, notification triggers and PVARA power to protect customers during exit.

26.7.3 Separating entity status from activity permission

72
A licence should identify the legal person, controlling owners, approved senior management, Pakistan establishment or responsible contact, permitted activities, customer classes, conditions, effective date and status. It should not operate as an undifferentiated permission to perform every Schedule I service. Each activity permission should be supported by demonstrated capital, governance, systems, staffing, outsourcing and wind-down capability.
73
The public register should use controlled statuses: NOC holder; transition applicant; application complete and under assessment; licensed with named permissions; licensed with restriction; suspended; surrender in progress; revoked; rejected; and exited. “Registered”, “approved” and “regulated” should not be used unless the underlying legal status justifies them. Historic entries should remain visible with dates so that a customer can determine the provider’s status when a transaction occurred.

26.7.4 A lawful transition matrix

74
PVARA should decide each inherited case through a published matrix rather than negotiate bespoke legal states. An existing NOC holder that files a complete and timely application may continue only within the section 70 bridge and applicable directions. A non-NOC existing provider must prove that it falls within the statutory class and meet the same completeness and protective floor; the existence of business before the Act is not itself an approval. A new entrant should not rely on section 70. An overseas provider targeting Pakistan should be classified under the statutory nexus and cross-border module rather than allowed to treat remote access as absence from the jurisdiction.
75
Where final Regulations are not in force when a statutory decision must be made, PVARA should identify which criteria arise directly from the Act and which cannot lawfully be improvised. The answer may be a narrow amendment or properly made interim instrument; it is not an unpublished scoring model. Applicants should receive a deficiency notice, reasonable opportunity to cure where law permits, and a reasoned determination. PVARA should publish service standards but avoid promising approval by a date irrespective of complexity.

26.7.5 Continuing fitness, ownership and governance

76
Fit-and-proper assessment should apply to beneficial owners, controllers, directors, key executives and persons controlling critical wallets or systems. It should examine integrity, competence, financial soundness, conflicts, regulatory history and capacity relevant to the role. Foreign findings should be verified and weighed, not automatically imported. A refusal based on confidential intelligence still requires a lawful method of giving sufficient substance to answer the case.
77
Change of control, material outsourcing, new activity, key-person departure, serious incident and financial deterioration should trigger notification or prior approval according to risk. Nominee arrangements and token-based governance must not obscure control. The provider should maintain an ownership-and-control map showing legal rights, practical influence, privileged access, treasury authority and dependencies. The Authority should retain an intervention and wind-down plan for every material provider.

26.7.6 Prudential requirements calibrated to loss channels

78
Capital should not be a single number copied across unlike businesses. The methodology should identify fixed overhead, custody exposure, credit and counterparty risk, settlement exposure, market risk, operational risk, group concentration and wind-down cost. Client assets do not count as the provider’s capital. Insurance may mitigate a defined loss but should not replace governance or liquid resources.
79
A low-footprint adviser may justify a different requirement from a custodian, exchange, lender or leveraged-derivative platform, yet each must hold resources sufficient for orderly exit. Thresholds and calculations should be public, with a power to impose an evidenced add-on and a right to reasons and review. The proof is not merely a submitted return: PVARA must reconcile capital to audited accounts, wallet and bank evidence, liabilities, group transfers and stress results.

26.7.7 Foreign reliance without automatic passporting

80
A foreign licence may reduce duplicated evidence where the home regime and supervisor can be verified. It should not automatically confer a Pakistan permission. PVARA must assess Pakistan nexus, local accountability, customer remedies, data and record access, asset location, insolvency consequences, supervisory cooperation and exit. Any equivalence or reliance determination should be published, scoped, time-limited and reviewable. Marketing into Pakistan should stop if the relied-on status lapses or effective cooperation fails.
81
Licensing completion requires a searchable register, consistent reasons, review notices, regulatory interfaces, assigned supervision, asset verification, incident escalation and an exercised exit. Licence volume alone proves little.

26.8 Monetary, Banking and Market Perimeters

26.8.1 Joint SBP–SECP–PVARA determination

82
Classification disputes should be resolved through a published protocol. PVARA can determine its own perimeter; SBP retains banking, monetary, payments and exchange-control functions; SECP retains securities, companies and relevant market functions. A joint determination should state facts, governing provisions, each body's conclusion and appeal route. Coordination must not become an unreviewable fourth regulator.

26.8.2 Stablecoins, tokenised assets and lawful banking access

83
Stablecoins require analysis of issuer, reserves, redemption, unit of account, payment use, cross-border movement and failure. Tokenised shares, debt or funds retain their underlying legal character. Banking access should be risk-based and transparent: neither automatic exclusion nor compulsory service without lawful authority. Banks need permitted-activity, capital, custody, AML and outsourcing rules from SBP.

26.8.3 A joint perimeter mechanism

84
PVARA, SBP and SECP should adopt a protocol that routes a product to the competent decision-maker without transferring legal power. The intake should capture: legal issuer; holder’s claim; reserve and redemption; payment use; return or profit right; governance and control; custody; transfer and settlement; marketing; Pakistan nexus; and failure allocation. Each regulator then records whether its law applies, what permission is required and which unresolved point needs primary correction.
85
The resulting perimeter determination should name the lead regulator for each decision and list concurrent permissions. A PVARA VASP licence should not be described as SBP approval for payment or foreign-exchange activity, SECP approval of a security or fund, or validation of title to an underlying asset. Conversely, an SBP or SECP permission should not dispense with a VAA permission where a professional virtual-asset service is performed.

26.8.4 Regulating stablecoin functions rather than labels

86
A token promoted as stable may embody a payment claim, stored value, debt, fund-like interest, contractual redemption promise, algorithmic mechanism or no enforceable stabilisation claim at all. The rulebook should classify these functions and reserve regulatory names for products meeting specified conditions. Before public distribution or admission, the responsible authority should identify the issuer, governing law, holder’s legal claim, reserve owner and custodian, permitted reserve assets, valuation, concentration, reconciliation, audit, redemption timing, fees, suspension conditions and insolvency treatment.47
87
Where the token is used for payment, settlement or conversion with Pakistani rupees, SBP’s payment, banking and foreign-exchange perimeter must be applied. PVARA may supervise the VASP and virtual-asset conduct; it cannot authorise the issue of currency or override foreign-exchange law. Where the token is a security, derivative or collective investment, SECP’s perimeter applies. A joint Financial-Referenced Token module should specify the hand-off, not invent a category outside the statutes.
88
Algorithmic stability requires a different control. The issuer or operator should disclose the mechanism, dependencies, governance keys, intervention authority, collateral if any, historical and simulated stress, oracle dependence, death spiral and wind-down. Distribution to retail customers should not proceed merely because a model held its target in benign conditions. The regulator should have authority to limit scale, require a sandbox, mandate warnings, order redemption or exit where promised, and halt misleading “stable” claims.

26.8.5 A lawful security-token and real-world-asset bridge

89
Tokenisation does not itself transfer shares, debt, land, warehouse goods or beneficial ownership. The corrective package should require a legal-rights memorandum for any security token or real-world-asset token: the underlying right; issuer and obligor; registry or title system; perfection and priority; custodian or trustee; corporate and transfer restrictions; enforcement; insolvency; and reconciliation between the authoritative legal register and the token ledger.
90
PVARA and SECP should jointly publish classification examples and an intake route. Where another registrar or provincial property system is legally authoritative, the blockchain should be treated as evidence or an operating layer only to the extent that law recognises it. A smart contract cannot cure a defective conveyance. Pilot approval should be conditional on a tested correction process for lost keys, erroneous transfers, corporate actions, court orders and divergence between ledgers.

26.9 Property, Custody and Customer Protection

26.9.1 Statutory recognition, segregation and reconciliation

91
Primary law should recognise digital assets as capable of supporting property rights without freezing future categories. Custodians should maintain customer-by-customer entitlements, segregate assets and money, reconcile internal and external records, control sub-custody, disclose reuse and prohibit unauthorised encumbrance. Proof of reserves must be paired with liabilities and legal title.

26.9.2 Insolvency portability, complaints and compensation

92
Customer assets should be excluded from the custodian's estate where held for customers, subject to a clear shortfall and cost rule. Portability to another licensed custodian should be possible where keys and records permit. Complaints need time limits, reasons and escalation. Any compensation scheme requires primary funding authority, coverage limits, governance and exclusions; it should not be promised before it exists.

26.9.3 Customer assets, market integrity and failure management

93
The customer-protection programme must make section 24 real at the point of failure. Disclosure is necessary, but it cannot substitute for legal segregation, control evidence, reconciliation and return. The central question is whether a customer can recover the correct asset, or legally determined equivalent, when a provider, bank, custodian, sub-custodian, protocol or key controller fails.48

26.9.4 The safeguarding chain

94
Final Regulations should require a customer-asset architecture approved for each custodial activity. It should identify wallets and bank accounts; legal and beneficial ownership; persons capable of initiating, approving and broadcasting a transfer; hot, warm and cold controls; key generation, storage, backup, rotation and recovery; sub-custodians; settlement windows; and the data reconciling each customer entitlement to assets on-chain and off-chain.
95
Reconciliation should occur at a frequency matched to risk and after material events. Differences require categorisation, investigation, customer-protective funding where appropriate and notification above defined thresholds. A proof-of-reserves publication, if permitted, should not be described as proof of solvency: it may omit liabilities, borrowed assets, encumbrances, off-chain positions and control risk. Independent assurance should examine both assets and liabilities, ownership, control and the procedure used.
96
Customer terms should state whether assets are held in individual or omnibus form, whether sub-custody is used, governing law, forks and airdrops, staking or lending, fees, insurance, error correction, death or incapacity, complaints and insolvency treatment. Any reuse must be separately elected and recorded. A general platform consent should not convert safeguarded custody into unsecured lending.

26.9.5 An executable recovery and wind-down plan

97
Each custodian, exchange and lender should maintain a recovery and wind-down plan. It should map critical staff, keys, vendors, banks, nodes, data, customer communications and asset-transfer capacity; identify triggers; and estimate resources for reconciliation and return. PVARA should be able to require a solvent wind-down before insolvency, appoint or seek appointment of an appropriate administrator where authorised, and coordinate with company courts and liquidators.
98
The plan should be exercised through a failure simulation. A provider must demonstrate that an alternate authorised group can reconstruct entitlements, secure keys, stop unauthorised transfers, communicate with customers, honour lawful freezes and return assets. The exercise should include a shortfall and a disputed claim. The result, deficiencies and remediation date should form part of supervisory evidence.

26.9.6 A complete market-integrity rulebook

99
The Act’s market-abuse and disclosure provisions require detailed definitions, evidentiary rules and surveillance expectations. Regulations should address wash trading, matched orders, spoofing, layering, ramping, marking, pump-and-dump conduct, misuse of confidential information, front running, manipulative token supply or oracle inputs, misleading liquidity, undisclosed paid promotion and conflicts in listing or proprietary trading.
100
Operators should maintain order, trade, wallet, communication, listing and beneficial-control records capable of reconstructing conduct across on-chain and off-chain venues. Surveillance should combine rules and human review, document model changes and prevent a provider from treating every anomaly as guilt. Referral criteria should identify when PVARA, SECP, FMU, FIA/NCCIA or another competent body becomes responsible. Parallel interest does not authorise unrestricted data circulation.

26.9.7 Complaints, compensation and remedy

101
Every licensed provider should operate a time-bound complaint process with acknowledgement, investigation, reasons, correction and escalation. PVARA should publish aggregate categories and outcomes, not merely accept portal submissions. The official grievance portal is useful administrative access, but it does not itself prove statutory adjudication, compensation authority or an operational Tribunal.49
102
The primary and delegated package should identify available remedies: correction of records, return of assets, reversal where legally and technically possible, compensation, fee refund, contract remedy, restitution, administrative direction, sanction and access to court. A compensation or fidelity arrangement should be adopted only after defining funding, coverage, exclusions, governance and relationship with insolvency. It must not socialise ordinary investment losses or imply State guarantee.

26.10 Financial Integrity and Enforcement Capability

26.10.1 AMLA allocation, Travel Rule and sanctions

103
The Anti-Money Laundering Act 2010, PVARA statute and Regulations should state which entities are reporting entities, which supervisor acts, and how Travel Rule information is obtained, transmitted and protected. Targeted financial sanctions require timely screening and freezing under the governing instruments. Analytics scores support risk assessment but do not replace designation, investigation or reasons.

26.10.2 Investigation, digital evidence and proportionate inclusion

104
Effective enforcement needs preservation orders, forensic custody, provider records, trained investigators and prosecutor-court competence. Broad account closures can push activity into less visible channels. Risk-tiered due diligence, transaction limits and enhanced review can protect inclusion while maintaining source-of-funds and suspicious-transaction obligations. Coercive action must remain lawful, reasoned and reviewable.

26.10.3 Enforcement capability, digital evidence and proportionate inclusion

105
The reform programme needs a named owner for every decision and an escalation route for every blocked dependency. The VAA Board’s multi-agency composition assists coordination, but the Board is not a device for exercising the statutory powers of its members’ institutions. Each participating body should sign an institutional responsibility protocol that identifies the legal source, decision owner, required contributors, protected-information limits, service standard, record, escalation and review route.55

26.10.4 Completing the AML/CFT legal allocation

106
The Federal Government should verify and publish the operative AMLA Schedule IV or other lawful designation connecting PVARA and covered virtual-asset reporting entities to the statutory supervisory architecture. PVARA, FMU and the relevant AML authorities should then issue an allocation protocol covering registration, risk assessment, inspection, enforcement referral, suspicious transaction reporting, sanctions, confidentiality, feedback and protected information. The protocol should track legal powers; it should not make FMU an ordinary data service or give PVARA access to protected material without authority.
107
PVARA should publish a sectoral risk assessment distinguishing exchange, transfer, custody, broking, lending, issuance, mining-related activity, DeFi interfaces, privacy-enhancing technology and cross-border corridors. Regulatory requirements and supervisory frequency should follow the evidence. Small size is not automatically low risk; high transaction value is not the only indicator.

26.10.5 Operationalising the Travel Rule

108
A binding standard should define covered transfers, originator and beneficiary information, verification, message timing, counterparty-VASP due diligence, secure transmission, missing or inconsistent data, rejected or suspended transfers, record retention and error correction. It should be technology-neutral and interoperable. The standard must also specify the risk-based process for self-hosted addresses: ownership or control evidence where proportionate, purpose and counterparty risk, sanctions screening, transaction monitoring, enhanced due diligence and refusal criteria. It should not impose a categorical ban without legislative authority and evidence.51
109
Implementation proof requires end-to-end tests between providers and across borders. The test set should include a normal transfer, missing fields, name mismatch, sanctioned party, high-risk corridor, self-hosted address, technical outage, corrected record and lawful information request. Supervisors should measure completeness, accuracy, latency, false positives, disposition and protected-data access—not count installed software.

26.10.6 Integrating targeted financial sanctions

110
The rulebook should require current sanctions data, customer and beneficial-owner screening, wallet and transaction analytics appropriate to risk, escalation, freezing or rejection where law requires, reporting, controlled release and false-positive correction. It should identify the legal instrument that creates the obligation and the authority capable of directing action. A commercial risk score is evidence for assessment, not legal proof that a person is designated.
111
Providers and PVARA should maintain an auditable chain from the applicable designation through match logic, human decision, action, notice where permitted, report and review. Where secrecy is lawfully required, the system should still preserve a record for independent oversight and eventual challenge.

26.11 Fiscal Architecture and Existing Holdings

26.11.1 Express classification, valuation and reporting

112
Tax legislation should determine income and capital treatment, business inventory, mining or validation receipts, staking, airdrops, forks, gifts, loss, source, valuation dates, withholding and information reporting. PVARA may supply market data but cannot create a tax charge. FBR should publish valuation hierarchy and record expectations for on-chain and off-chain transactions.

26.11.2 Treatment of prior holdings, P2P evidence and any enacted regularisation route

113
Prior holdings need opening-basis, acquisition-date and source-of-funds rules that reflect imperfect records without inviting fabrication. Acceptable evidence can include bank records, platform exports, signed messages, wallet history and contemporaneous correspondence, assessed together. Any amnesty or disclosure programme must be enacted by competent authority and should not be inferred from licensing transition.

26.11.3 A federal tax taxonomy through fiscal law

114
FBR should publish, and where necessary seek legislation for, a coherent event-based framework. It should address acquisition, disposal, exchange, payment, mining rewards, staking or validation rewards, airdrops, forks, lending yield, liquidity provision, derivatives, token issuance, employee compensation, gifts, inheritance, theft, loss and migration between personal wallets. For each event it should identify the taxpayer, head of income or gain, recognition time, basis, valuation source, deductible cost, loss treatment, records and withholding or reporting obligation.
115
The framework should distinguish accounting from tax and personal investment from business activity without relying on token labels. Valuation rules need a hierarchy for exchange-traded, thinly traded, non-convertible and related-party assets, with methods for absent or manipulated prices. Correcting section 66’s title is part of textual hygiene; it is not the tax reform.
116
Domestic reporting should precede any promise of automatic international exchange. FBR should define reporting entities, reportable users and assets, due diligence, data fields, correction, retention, confidentiality, enforcement and taxpayer challenge in tax law. The OECD Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework may inform design, but it does not apply itself in Pakistan. Treaty authority, domestic legislation, systems, security and an activated exchange relationship must exist before data is sent.

26.11.4 Cooperative resolution of provincial services-tax treatment

117
Virtual-asset services may engage provincial sales taxes on services while other fiscal consequences remain federal. The Federation and provinces should use the constitutional coordination machinery and revenue-authority cooperation to publish a common service map: exchange, brokerage, advisory, custody, management, transfer, lending facilitation, issuance services, mining pools and technology supply. The map should identify place-of-supply, business and customer location, inter-provincial service, input treatment, registration, invoicing and audit coordination.54
118
Harmonisation should not mean an executive transfer of taxing power. Each legislature or authorised revenue body must make the necessary rule within its competence. A common return or portal can reduce duplication only if its legal basis, data custody, assessment responsibility, payment allocation and appeal routes are clear. The implementation test is a set of transactions assessed consistently by FBR and each participating provincial authority, with no double collection and no unreviewable gap.

26.12 Data, Cybersecurity and Digital Identity

26.12.1 General data law and sectoral duties

119
Pakistan needs a coherent general data-protection baseline supplemented by sectoral financial duties. Providers should collect only necessary identity and transaction data, state purpose and retention, secure transfers and enable correction. Public chains should not receive personal data merely to simplify compliance. Foreign storage and analytics need contractual and legal controls.

26.12.2 Operational resilience, identity and supervisory data

120
Licensing should require board responsibility for cyber risk, key management, change control, testing, incident response, outsourcing and recovery. Digital identity can support reusable verification but must prevent exclusion and function creep. Supervisory reporting should use defined data fields, quality rules and access logs. Providers remain responsible for outsourced services.

26.12.3 General data law and an interim sectoral bridge

121
Pakistan needs comprehensive primary data-protection legislation establishing lawful processing, rights, controller and processor duties, sensitive-data safeguards, cross-border transfer rules, independent oversight, enforcement and remedies. Until that is enacted, PVARA should use the VAA, PECA, sectoral confidentiality duties, contract, constitutional rights and lawful Regulation powers to impose a bounded Virtual Asset Data Code without pretending that subordinate rules constitute a general national data law.52
122
The Code should require purpose specification, minimisation, accuracy, retention, access control, encryption, logging, processor governance, breach response, customer access and correction, and rules for regulatory disclosure. It should separate onboarding data, transaction monitoring, Travel Rule messages, biometric material, wallet analytics, complaints and enforcement evidence. Cross-border outsourcing should be conditional on legal access, security, deletion and regulator cooperation, not localisation rhetoric alone.

26.12.4 Operational resilience and incident rules

123
PVARA should define critical services and impact tolerances, not merely recovery-time promises. Providers should map assets, keys, systems, people, vendors, cloud regions, banks, nodes, oracles and data flows; conduct threat-led tests proportionate to risk; manage vulnerabilities and changes; and maintain secure recovery. Material incidents should be classified by customer harm, asset loss, service disruption, data compromise, integrity failure and cross-sector effect.
124
The reporting standard should provide an immediate alert for critical incidents, a structured initial report, updates and a root-cause and remediation report. Timing should begin when the provider reasonably determines the classification, with controls against deliberate delay. PVARA should operate a 24-hour intake and coordinate with the competent cybercrime, data, financial and security bodies under a lawful protocol. Customer notice should be prompt unless a legally authorised delay is necessary to contain harm or protect an investigation.
125
Completion is demonstrated by exercises: compromised signing key; cloud-region outage; corrupted entitlement ledger; oracle manipulation; ransomware; insider exfiltration; and failure of the Travel Rule vendor. The exercise should test authority, decisions, evidence custody, customer communication, asset security, alternate processing and recovery—not only technical restoration.

131
The Tribunal requires a constitutionally defensible primary route and an operational package. 26.13.6 Connecting administrative redress to legal remedy

133
PVARA’s complaint channel should triage: provider complaint; supervisory intelligence; urgent asset or cyber event; alleged crime; tax or payment issue; and appealable PVARA decision. The customer should be told which route applies and what the portal cannot decide. Complaints about a provider should ordinarily go first to the provider unless urgency, conflict or futility justifies direct escalation. Complaints about PVARA require a structurally separate handling route and must preserve statutory and constitutional remedies.
134
The Authority should publish quarterly data on receipt, subject, provider response, time, outcome, systemic finding, referrals and outstanding age, with lawful privacy protection. It should identify repeated causes and use them in supervision. A high closure rate is not success if cases are administratively closed without correction or remedy.

26.13.7 Resourcing and holding institutions accountable

135
The capacity plan should cover legal drafting, authorisation, prudential and custody analysis, surveillance, AML/CFT, cyber, data, enforcement, economics, tax and records. It should identify build, train, second, procure and shared-service choices, with conflict and vendor-dependence controls.
136
PVARA’s annual plan should connect resources to application quality, risk-based supervision, asset exceptions, incidents, AML remediation, complaints, enforcement, appeals and unresolved interagency decisions. Licence count, volume and investment are context, not proof of regulatory success.

26.15 Human-Governed Machine-Readable Regulation

26.15.1 Rules as code without fictional institutions or automated authority

142
Pakistan may publish structured representations of definitions, permissions, reporting fields and decision tests once the authoritative legal text and institutional ownership exist. A model can support forms, consistency checks and supervised compliance. It does not become law, issue a licence, decide an appeal or create a new institution. The legal source authorises; the representation assists; a competent human body decides.

26.15.2 Traceability, explanation, contestability and controlled change

143
Every machine-readable rule should link to the exact provision, version and effective date. Outputs should disclose inputs, rule path and confidence or unresolved issues. A person must be able to correct data and obtain human reasons. Changes should pass legal approval and testing before deployment, with rollback and historical reproducibility.

26.15.3 From authenticated law to reviewable implementation

144
Pakistan’s digital-assets problem is no longer the absence of a primary statute. The Virtual Assets Act 2026 supplies an operative authority, perimeter, licensing architecture, direct duties, enforcement powers, delegated-law structure and appeal pathway. The harder problem is implementation across five different objects: law, policy, administrative process, data and code. A statute states legal authority and obligations. Policy selects objectives. A process allocates work and decision points. Data records facts or claims about facts. Code performs defined operations on supplied inputs. A system becomes unsafe when it treats one object as proof of another.
145
A scanned Act is digital but not structured. Structured text is searchable but not necessarily machine-readable at provision level. A machine-readable provision is not necessarily executable. Executable logic is not necessarily legally correct. A technically correct implementation of a rule may still be used by an institution without power, applied to the wrong legal version, fed incomplete evidence, or allowed to produce a result that only a human officer or tribunal can lawfully decide.
146
Those errors have different owners and remedies. A drafting ambiguity may require an amending instrument. A mistaken model requires legal correction and regression testing. Poor evidence requires verification or a finding that the fact is not proved. An unauthorised decision requires institutional and legal redress even if the calculation was flawless. Treating every failure as an “IT issue” conceals the public body responsible for correcting it.
147
The target is therefore not “law replaced by software”. It is a governed chain from authenticated legal source to a transparent model, bounded test, reasoned administrative file and reviewable human act. Computation can expose contradiction, ask consistent questions, validate dates or document fields, reproduce a formula and route a case. It can also reveal where the law is incomplete or depends on judgment. It must not convert that incompleteness into an invented rule.
148

This chapter turns that requirement into an architecture. Its controlling rule is simple: the official legal instrument authorises; the model represents; the program assists; the competent person decides where law or fact requires judgment.

150

It is not a new legal order and, at the present stage, is not a deployed public system. Its purpose is to preserve the path from source to implementation in a form usable by lawyers, drafters, regulators, service designers, engineers, regulated persons and reviewers.
151

In an operative pathway, either label must stop the affected conclusion and route the matter to an authorised human process.

26.15.6 From anticipatory indicators to executable representation

168
the machine-readable-regulation programme should express the dashboard in machine-consumable form without allowing code to decide legal authority. Each indicator requires: stable identifier; source; status; effective version; data owner; condition; evidence or data-quality limitation—not scenario probability; accountable institution; lawful actions; mandatory consultation; safeguard; evidence; reasons; remedy; expiry and review. [DRAFT] and [GAP] states must block a system from presenting a proposed or absent rule as operative.
169
Agent identity and authorisation are suitable technical tests; statutory interpretation, proportionality, contested facts, institutional competence and remedy remain human legal judgments. International AI frameworks can inform governance, mapping, measurement and deactivation, but they do not allocate Pakistani power.81 The machine-readable result should therefore generate a traceable alert and decision file, not an automatic freeze, licence refusal, tax charge or finding of liability.
170
The chapter’s conclusion is conditional but firm. Pakistan does not need to know which scenario will occur in order to make authority visible, protect customer assets, connect regulators, preserve evidence, test remedies and watch concentration. Those are no-regret foundations. The future question is not whether code, stablecoins, tokenisation or AI will inevitably transform finance. It is whether the legal order can recognise a material change early, identify the competent decision-maker, act within law, distribute loss fairly and revise itself on evidence.

26.15.7 Law as Code, Rules as Code and the authority boundary

171
“Law as Code” and “Rules as Code” are useful only if their meanings are separated. Law as Code is the broader inquiry into how legal norms can be represented, tested or implemented computationally and how code itself structures conduct. Rules as Code is a public-administration method: selected rules are expressed in machine-consumable form, ideally through a multidisciplinary process connected to policy and legislative drafting. Neither phrase establishes that software is law.
172

The OECD’s 2020 working paper describes Rules as Code as an official machine-consumable version produced alongside human-readable rules and treats it as a change in rulemaking, not merely a software project. It also records limitations and competing conceptions rather than presenting one mature global standard.82 New Zealand’s Better Rules discovery similarly used multidisciplinary teams and tested concept models, decision models and rule statements. Its important negative finding was that not all legislation is suitable for machine consumption.83 These are comparative foundations, not sources of Pakistani authority.
174
The distinction between machine-readable, machine-consumable and machine-executable should be maintained. A provision with a stable identifier, dates and subject tags is machine-readable. A structured obligation that a service can query is machine-consumable. A rule that evaluates inputs and returns a result is executable. Each transition adds interpretation and therefore adds a new point at which error, institutional bias or silent legal change can enter.
175
The terms describe capability, not authority.
176
Standardised identifiers and metadata can improve access without purporting to decide cases. The European Legislation Identifier, for example, uses persistent identifiers and structured metadata so legislation can be accessed, exchanged and reused across systems.84 Pakistan can learn from that publication architecture without importing European law or assuming that linked data resolves statutory interpretation.
177
Three propositions follow.
178
First, official status is a legal question. A government-hosted file may be a consultation draft, guidance, a consolidated convenience copy or an operative instrument. A machine must carry that status, not infer authority from a domain name. Secondly, equivalence between text and model cannot be self-declared by the developer. It requires legal mapping, test cases and approval within the relevant institution’s competence. Thirdly, even an officially published computational model would require an express rule about its legal effect. Until Parliament or valid delegated legislation says otherwise, the authenticated legal text and competent decision remain controlling.
179
This treatise therefore uses a deliberately bounded formulation: controlled legal knowledge architecture may make legal requirements more legible, traceable and testable. It does not make public power self-executing. “Code is law” is rejected both descriptively and constitutionally. Code can constrain a workflow, but only law can supply the State’s authority to burden a person, and only an authorised process can turn proved facts into an adverse public decision.

185
Temporal logic deserves separate testing. Publication, commencement, transition, savings, repeal and retrospective effect are not interchangeable dates. A consolidated text may be convenient for present questions but may obscure the provision governing an earlier transaction. The decision file must preserve the version applied to the relevant event and the legal reason for applying it. Where a court has interpreted a provision, the model should link the decision and its precedential status without converting a fact-sensitive holding into a universal Boolean rule.

26.15.10 Regulatory data, interoperability, privacy and evidence

193
A rule model is only as reliable as its source and input data. Interoperability should therefore begin with identifiers and meaning, not bulk data transfer. The system needs stable identifiers for instruments, provisions, organisations, licences, permissions, officers, applications, evidence items, decisions, cases and model releases. Each shared field requires a definition, lawful purpose, source custodian, validation rule, time quality and correction route.
194
The Digital Nation Pakistan Act defines digital public infrastructure to include identity, data exchange, cloud and open-data components and gives the Pakistan Digital Authority important standards and coordination functions. It also preserves control and responsibility of designated data custodians and expressly requires avoidance of overlap with other regulatory mandates.88 That supports a common technical layer; it does not create permission for PVARA, PDA or a vendor to collect or combine any dataset that would be useful.
195
The data architecture should use minimum disclosure. An eligibility or perimeter query often requires verification of a proposition rather than disclosure of the underlying record. A system may need to know that a company exists and that a representative holds a valid role, not retrieve every filing or personal identifier. Access should be role-based and purpose-limited; sensitive evidence should be encrypted, logged and segregated; test data should be synthetic or lawfully de-identified; and public rule models should not expose investigative thresholds whose disclosure would defeat a lawful function.
196
Chapter 13 established that data protection, cybersecurity and evidence are separate legal questions. A secure database can still be unlawful; a lawful collection can still be insecure; and an accurate system log can still require proof of attribution and integrity. controlled legal knowledge architecture should preserve the source of an input, who supplied or changed it, when it was valid, the method of verification and any contest. Hashes and digital signatures can assist integrity and authentication, but they cannot prove that the original factual assertion was true.89
197
Evidence exports must be intelligible without the live system. A decision file should contain the operative source text or stable official locator, all relevant versions, inputs, provenance, branch path, reasons, human approvals, notices and later changes in a durable format. Retention must follow legal purpose and proceedings, not the convenience of indefinite storage. A person should be able to correct an erroneous factual record without rewriting the historical fact that the earlier decision used it.
198
Interoperability also requires graceful failure. If an identity, company, tax, sanctions or regulator interface is unavailable, the service should show the failure and offer a lawful alternative. “No response from another database” must not be translated into “not eligible” or “non-compliant”. Cross-agency APIs should carry status and timestamp, not just values, and each institution must remain responsible for the field it authoritatively maintains.

26.15.11 Human authority, explanation and contestability

199
Human control is not satisfied by placing an officer after an automated result if the officer lacks time, evidence, training or authority to depart from it. Meaningful human judgment requires the power to ask for more information, change a classification, refuse the model’s recommendation, record reasons and trigger correction of a defective rule. The reviewer must know which parts were calculated and which were legal or factual assumptions.
200
The workflow must also guard against automation bias. A prominently displayed score or “recommended outcome” may become functionally decisive even when a form permits override. Assurance should therefore examine actual override rates, time available for review, whether officers receive the underlying evidence rather than only the output, and whether departures attract inappropriate managerial pressure. For contested or adverse cases, the interface should present the legal question and evidence before any system recommendation, so that the officer forms an independent view.
201
The Constitution requires public power to be exercised through law, fair process and legally responsible institutions. The VAA allocates classification and licensing functions to PVARA, requires written reasons for refusal at the NOC stage and provides an appellate structure for persons aggrieved by Authority orders.90 Software cannot become the statutory Authority, an appellate tribunal or the Supreme Court. Nor can a generic “human in the loop” transfer one regulator’s power to another institution.

203
Explanation should track the actual decision. A generic statement that “requirements were not met” is not cured by attaching hundreds of rules. The officer should identify the decisive legal provision, factual finding, evidence accepted or rejected, treatment of a material representation and consequence selected. Where security or investigation law justifies withholding particular information, the limitation itself needs authority and review; secrecy should not become the default explanation for system opacity.
204
No automated tool should make or execute an adverse legal decision merely because its prediction is accurate on historical data. Prediction and authority are different. Historical outcomes may encode inconsistent administration, exclusion or error. A machine-learning score may help prioritise a review only under an approved purpose and assurance regime; it should not supply an undisclosed substantive criterion. For the bounded programme proposed here, the preferable core is transparent deterministic logic, with analytics kept separate and never allowed to overwrite the legal rule path.
205
Contestability also improves the model. Successful challenges, withdrawn decisions and unusual cases should enter the regression suite after legal review. That does not mean that a single officer’s decision rewrites the law. It means the programme tests whether the model omitted an exception, misrepresented an ambiguity, relied on poor data or invited users to answer a legal conclusion as if it were a simple fact.
206
A remedy must remain usable outside the platform. Loss of an account, service outage or inaccessible interface cannot extinguish a statutory time limit or right to make representations. Notices should identify an alternative channel, and the complete record should be obtainable in a durable human-readable form. The reviewing institution should be able to suspend reliance on a disputed model without disabling the underlying legal process.

212
Procurement cannot outsource responsibility. A contract should require open data and rule formats where lawful, documentation, source and dependency disclosure, portability, security obligations, incident notice, audit access, exit assistance and continued access to decision records. Proprietary code must not prevent PVARA, a tribunal or a court from understanding the basis of a public decision. Vendor performance claims and laboratory accuracy are not evidence of lawful deployment.
213
Governance should include conflicts and participation. Regulated firms may properly test whether a rule model reflects operational reality, but they should not control the interpretation, acceptance tests or release decision for requirements that bind them. Consultation evidence should be published or summarised with reasons, while confidential and security-sensitive material remains protected under identified authority.
214

Material incidents include not only cyber compromise but use of a draft as operative law, a stale model, inability to explain a result, systematic override by staff, data drift, inaccessible review and any automatic adverse consequence outside authority.
215
Programme metrics should measure legal and administrative quality: source defects found, unresolved gaps, disagreement categories, corrected inputs, time and cost to complete the lawful process, successful challenges, security events and remedy availability. A high number of automated completions is not success if people cannot understand or contest them. The assurance function should report residual defects and have a defined suspension threshold.

26.15.14 A five-phase controlled demonstration programme

222
The programme should advance by evidence gates, not by announcing a national platform and filling it later.
223
Phase 0 — source integrity. Establish authenticated source registers for the VAA, valid subordinate instruments and directly connected law. Assign provision identifiers, status, commencement, amendment and official-location metadata. Publicly separate operative, draft, historical and missing material. No executable case outcome is required.
224
Phase 1 — controlled research prototype. Refactor the existing perimeter screen into a self-contained, reproducible package. Replace legal-conclusion checkboxes with human determination objects. Add missing-input, draft, gap, cross-regulator and source-change cases. Publish the rule map, limitations and test results as research. Success is faithful traceability and safe escalation, not approval accuracy.
225
Phase 2 — institutional shadow pilot. With PVARA and connected institutions’ approval, trained officers run the model alongside—not inside—the live process. It cannot affect an applicant. Compare its questions and routing with human files; record disagreement; test privacy, security, accessibility and record export; and verify that the real review route works.
226
Phase 3 — legal validation. Responsible institutions approve a bounded rule set, use, version and reasons protocol through the appropriate legal and administrative instruments. Independent reviewers test authority, source fidelity, edge cases, data and prohibited outcomes. Any [GAP] that controls the result prevents progression.
227
Phase 4 — bounded public assistance and scale decision. A public service may explain the perimeter, collect an evidence inventory and route a user to the competent process. It should not issue a compliance badge or licence. Scale occurs only after measured reduction in error or burden, no material rights or security defect, demonstrable human override, working remedies, sustainable ownership and a reasoned Commission/sector decision under existing law.
228
The stop rules are as important as the phases. Code must never decide whether Parliament conferred a power; resolve an unresolved conflict between statutes; determine a contested legal character solely from a label; find dishonesty or liability; impose a sanction; freeze property; grant, refuse, suspend or revoke a licence; decide proportionality; dispose of an appeal; or prevent a court from examining legality. It may assemble the record, identify the question, calculate a formally determinate element, display alternatives and expose missing proof. The judgment and legal act remain with the authorised human institution.
229
This boundary is not hostility to technology. It is the condition for trustworthy technology in public law. Code supplies precision, reproducibility and the capacity to test implementation. Law supplies authority, rights, limits, reasons and remedy. Capital supplies capability and exposure: the resources to build systems, the incentives that may distort them, and the losses distributed when they fail. Intelligent order is achieved when each is made visible to the others without being allowed to impersonate them.
230
Pakistan’s contribution need not be the largest automated regulatory platform. It can be more important: a model of machine-legible administration in which the source is authenticated, drafts cannot masquerade as law, gaps stop false certainty, every branch is traceable, institutions retain their lawful competence, adverse decisions remain reasoned and reviewable, and correction improves both text and implementation. That is how a National Digital Masterplan can move toward machine-executable regulation without making regulation machine-sovereign.

26.17.4 Baseline order: prolonged incompleteness

255
Assumption. The VAA remains operative, NOC and transition cases continue to require administration, final regulatory modules or connected instruments arrive unevenly, and a material share of customer activity remains offshore or outside a clearly evidenced domestic permission. This is a continuation scenario, not a claim that delay will occur.
256
The legal stress is produced by time. An NOC, pending application, sandbox permission, saved Ordinance-era instrument and statutory licence are different legal states. If the public register, completeness method, transition determination, appointment record and review route remain incomplete, repeated administrative practice may harden into market expectation without acquiring legal authority. Banks, customers and foreign counterparties may then rely on inconsistent descriptions of who is permitted to do what.
257
The affected interests are broader than applicants. Customers may believe that regulatory engagement equals full supervision. Compliant firms may bear higher costs than offshore competitors. Banks may either over-exclude legitimate applicants or supply access without a complete regulatory status. PVARA staff may be asked to decide under criteria not yet contained in valid instruments. A court may receive an urgent dispute before the Tribunal, record-transfer process or digital-asset custody mechanism is demonstrably operational.
258
Current law is not empty during this scenario. Direct statutory duties concerning unauthorised activity, customer assets, conduct, AML/CFT, cooperation and enforcement remain relevant according to their terms.64 The danger is selective operability: coercive powers may be invoked before corresponding procedures, reasons and remedies work; businesses may face duties without a usable application path; or customers may receive warnings without a practical return or complaint route.
259
The no-regret response is therefore the corrective programme’s Horizon I package: publish exact status; authenticate appointments and delegations; maintain the authoritative instruments register; verify the lawful review and investigation routes; decide section 74 continuity instrument by instrument; and state which requirements apply during transition. Government should progress only those measures for which authority and implementation capacity are proved. It should not compensate for delay by presenting a draft handbook as binding or by announcing scale targets.
260
Early indicators. The dashboard should watch the age and legal status of NOC and licence applications; number of publicly unclassified providers; frequency of banks requesting regulator confirmation; customer complaints caused by status confusion; unpublished or superseded documents still used in decisions; vacancies and unverified delegations; inability to file an appeal; and the proportion of supervisory actions later withdrawn for procedural reasons.
261
Residual risk and implication. Even a candid transition notice cannot eliminate litigation over accrued positions or the exact operation of section 70. The design implication is to preserve customer continuity while refusing legal fiction. If the baseline persists, the response is targeted amendment or a lawfully made instrument—not indefinite administrative tolerance. Uncertainty: neither the duration of incomplete implementation nor the scale of offshore substitution can be responsibly quantified from the verified record.

26.17.5 Regulated-market deepening

262
Assumption. The amendment, Rules and final Regulations contemplated by the corrective programme are substantially completed; PVARA grants activity-specific permissions; banks supply controlled account access; and supervised domestic providers begin to mediate a greater range of customer activity. This scenario tests success conditions rather than assuming that authorisation itself is success.
263
The first stress is supervisory load. A licence converts a prospective assessment into a continuing obligation to supervise ownership, governance, capital, custody, conduct, AML/CFT, technology, outsourcing and wind-down. If licence issuance outpaces trained staff, reliable returns and inspection capability, the system may create a stronger appearance of safety than the evidence warrants. The risk increases if a small number of exchanges, custodians, banks, cloud providers or analytics vendors become essential across the market.
264
The second stress is multi-function concentration. An exchange may combine brokerage, custody, lending, proprietary dealing, token listing, settlement, staking and issuance support. Legal-entity approval cannot erase conflicts between these functions. Customer assets may support settlement exposure; proprietary trading may influence listing or execution; and group entities may shift risk beyond the licensed perimeter. The FSB and IOSCO frameworks both treat functional coverage, conflicts, data and cross-border cooperation as necessary parts of implementation rather than consequences that follow automatically from licensing.65
265
The third stress is connected-law dependency. A PVARA permission cannot supply SBP payment or foreign-exchange authority, SECP securities authority, FBR tax treatment, provincial services-tax treatment, property title or an environmental approval. Market deepening therefore tests the joint perimeter and referral mechanism more severely than a small transitional market. A delayed decision by one institution can block a lawful product; an informal concurrence can expose all institutions to challenge.
266
the corrective programme remains resilient if licences carry a machine- and human-readable permission schedule; every provider has a named supervisory owner; capital and customer assets are independently reconciled; material outsourcing and group exposure are mapped; cross-regulator determinations are published; and an exercised wind-down route exists. Expansion should be sequenced by risk, with custody, exchange, lending, leverage, payment and issuer permissions subject to stronger evidence than low-footprint advice.
267
The market should not be measured by licence count alone. Early indicators include assets and customers per provider; share of custody, liquidity, cloud and banking dependencies concentrated in the largest entities; capital exceptions; reconciliation breaks; complaints per activity; unresolved perimeter referrals; frequency and age of prudential remediation; staff-to-risk ratios; and the proportion of providers capable of completing an exit exercise. A rapid increase in licensed volume combined with flat supervisory coverage is an escalation trigger, not a success indicator.
268
Residual risk and implication. Regulation can shift risk rather than remove it: into concentrated providers, shared infrastructure, group entities or customer belief in State guarantee. The legal design should therefore preserve resolution, portability and failure planning from the first licence. Uncertainty: the verified record does not establish the future number, scale, ownership or business model of licensed providers, or the degree to which Pakistani customers will move from offshore channels.

26.17.6 Stablecoin and payment stress

269
Assumption. One or more fiat- or asset-referenced tokens become materially used by Pakistani customers for conversion, remittance, merchant settlement, savings-like balances, DeFi collateral or cross-border trade. The scenario does not assume that such use is lawful in every form, that a token is legal tender, or that adoption reaches any numerical threshold.
270
Stablecoin scale compresses several legal events into a fast technical path: acquisition with rupees or foreign currency; custody or self-hosting; cross-border transfer; conversion; payment instruction; merchant settlement; reserve redemption; and accounting or tax recognition. Different institutions may own each decision. The VAA provides fiat- and asset-referenced categories and issuer/reserve architecture, but SBP retains currency, banking, payment and foreign-exchange authority under its laws. Contractual acceptance by a merchant does not convert the token into legal tender or settle the legality of the conversion and payment legs.
271
Three failure mechanisms should be tested. First, an issuer run may arise from doubt about reserve quality, custody, legal title, redemption, banking access or sanctions. Secondly, a gateway interruption may prevent conversion or merchant settlement even where the token continues to trade on-chain. Thirdly, a cross-border integrity shock may cause foreign issuers, banks or platforms to freeze addresses or restrict a jurisdiction, leaving customers with an asset they cannot lawfully or practically redeem.
272
The financial-integrity stress is substantial. FATF’s 2025 update reports increasing illicit use of stablecoins, continuing offshore-VASP challenges and weak recovery after major thefts; its revised Recommendation 16 also changes international payment-transparency expectations.66 These are global observations, not findings about Pakistani users. They justify a system capable of identifying originator, beneficiary, provider, self-hosted address risk and lawful freeze or rejection without treating every stablecoin transfer as illicit.
273
The monetary and prudential stress is different. BIS’s 2026 analysis recognises tokenisation’s potential while concluding that current stablecoin arrangements do not supply all foundational properties of money and may create integrity and macro-financial challenges at scale.67 For Pakistan, the relevant question is not whether a foreign report prefers another monetary architecture. It is whether reserve, redemption, liquidity, currency substitution, FX, settlement and loss allocation can be governed under Pakistani law.
274
the corrective programme’s FRT module should therefore be tested before material retail or payment scale. The test requires a legally identified issuer; holder claim; reserve ownership, custody, composition and valuation; daily or risk-based reconciliation; redemption time and suspension grounds; payment and FX permissions; customer-money treatment; settlement finality; refunds and chargebacks; AML/Travel Rule interoperability; insolvency; and a coordinated run plan. A reserve attestation without liability, control and redemption evidence is insufficient.
275
Early indicators. Authorities should monitor, under lawful data powers, stablecoin exposure by provider and use rather than publicity estimates; concentration by issuer, reserve custodian and blockchain; redemption requests and delay; deviations from the promised reference; changes in issuer terms; bank or gateway interruptions; use as DeFi collateral; self-hosted and cross-border transfer patterns; sanctions or freezing events; customer complaints about convertibility; and differences between on-chain supply evidence and provider liabilities.
276
Residual risk and implication. Pakistan cannot compel a foreign issuer, reserve bank or blockchain to continue service. Domestic rules can limit exposure, require local accountability, preserve evidence and create an exit route, but cannot guarantee parity. The legal design implication is to cap or condition retail, payment or public-sector reliance until redemption and failure tests pass, and to require SBP–PVARA–FMU coordination before crisis. Uncertainty: future stablecoin use, reserve location, issuer cooperation and the speed of a confidence shock are unknown; no adoption or loss figure is forecast.

26.17.7 Tokenised assets and capital-market expansion

277
Assumption. Regulated firms or public bodies pilot or offer tokenised shares, debt, fund interests, commodities, warehouse goods, land-related claims or other real-world assets. Demand may come from domestic investors, diaspora, institutions or cross-border platforms, but the scenario assigns no volume or success probability.
278
Tokenisation can reduce reconciliation friction and make transfer conditions programmable, but it also creates a second representation of a legal right. The central stress is divergence: the token ledger shows one holder while the company register, depository, land record, warehouse document, secured-transactions registry or custodian record shows another. A technically final transfer may be legally ineffective; a valid court order may not propagate on-chain; or an oracle may report that an off-chain condition occurred when it did not.
279
The applicable law follows the underlying right and function. A token representing a share or investment may engage securities, company, depository and market-infrastructure law. A token purporting to represent land or goods depends on the law governing title, registration, possession and priority. The VAA can govern issuance services, virtual-asset conduct and custody, but it does not make every tokenised claim valid or displace SECP, registrar, provincial or judicial authority.68
280
The stress chain should test six events: valid initial creation; disclosure and investor classification; transfer and settlement; corporate action or income distribution; creation and enforcement of a security interest; and issuer, custodian or platform insolvency. It should also test a lost key, erroneous transfer, fork, frozen wallet, fraudulent duplicate token and mismatch between token supply and legally outstanding rights.
281
the corrective programme’s issuer and RWA bridge remains adequate only if every project has a legal-rights memorandum and an authoritative-record design. The memorandum should identify issuer and obligor, holder’s enforceable claim, governing law, authoritative register, transfer formalities, restrictions, custody, perfection, priority, corporate actions, tax, enforcement and insolvency. The system should specify when the token ledger is authoritative, evidential or merely operational, and who can lawfully correct divergence.
282
International work supplies evidence of possibilities and preconditions, not Pakistani authority. BIS work on tokenisation identifies potential use in payments and financial transactions and emphasises the legal, governance and operational foundations needed for deployment.69 The correct domestic pilot therefore links technical tests to a recognised asset and institutional owner. A sandbox decision cannot validate title, waive public procurement, or substitute for a securities approval.
283
Early indicators. The dashboard should record asset classes proposed; classification time; percentage of pilots with complete legal-rights memoranda; authoritative-register integration; reconciliation exceptions; corporate actions requiring manual correction; rejected or reversed transfers; custody concentration; secondary-market liquidity; investor complaints; and unresolved questions of perfection, tax or jurisdiction. A project should stop if the underlying registry cannot recognise or correct the tokenised state.
284
Residual risk and implication. Some legal rights may not be suitable for tokenisation without primary amendment or registry reform. Parallel records can increase, rather than reduce, operational complexity. The design implication is “registry before token”: identify the legal source of truth and correction authority before public issuance. Uncertainty: it is unknown which asset classes will present a lawful cost advantage over existing electronic systems, and no efficiency or capital-raising outcome is assumed.

26.17.8 DeFi, DAO and autonomous-agent diffusion

285
Assumption. Pakistani customers obtain material access to decentralised exchanges, lending pools, staking arrangements, DAO-governed services or AI systems that recommend, initiate or manage virtual-asset transactions. Access may occur through local interfaces, foreign applications, wallets, APIs or general-purpose agents. The scenario does not assume that a protocol is decentralised merely because its code is public or its governance uses tokens.
286
The first stress is factual attribution. A protocol may involve developers, deployers, interface operators, governance participants, oracle providers, administrators, multisignature signers, liquidity suppliers, fee recipients and users. Their legal significance differs. IOSCO’s DeFi work treats claims of decentralisation as facts to be examined through control, influence, economic interest and operational roles, while preserving the need to apply the relevant legal framework.70 Pakistan should use those indicators to investigate, not to impose automatic developer or token-holder liability.
287
The second stress is authority. An automated system may form or perform a contract, route an order, rebalance assets, vote, borrow, liquidate collateral or transmit a payment. Pakistan’s Electronic Transactions Ordinance supports electronic form and attribution of automated communications to a person using or controlling the system, but attribution does not resolve consent, authority, mistake, fiduciary obligation, market conduct or remedy. The VAA’s functional definitions and control concepts may reach a service or issuer without conferring personality on code.71
288
An AI agent sharpens this problem because it may select tools and actions not individually specified by the user. A provider could describe the output as information while the system actually personalises advice, exercises discretion or executes transactions. A customer may give a broad natural-language instruction that the agent converts into a chain of swaps, approvals and protocol interactions. The legal analysis must separate the user’s mandate, deployer’s design, model provider, wallet permission, interface, execution venue and any human override.
289
The third stress is common-mode behaviour. If many firms use the same model, data source, cloud service, oracle or agent framework, apparently independent decisions may become correlated. The FSB identifies third-party concentration, market correlation, cyber risk, model risk, data quality and governance as potential financial-system vulnerabilities from AI adoption.72 In digital-asset markets, those vulnerabilities can combine with continuous trading, automated liquidation and thin liquidity.
290
the corrective programme’s functional perimeter remains resilient if final rules require an automated-system responsibility file. It should identify the legally accountable deployer; permitted task; customer mandate; activity classification; data and model dependencies; wallet and API permissions; transaction and value limits; testing; monitoring; override and shutdown; record of prompts, policy and actions where lawful; incident response; customer explanation; and remedy. The file should be proportionate, protect trade secrets and personal data, and preserve sufficient evidence to contest a decision.
291
DeFi regulation should focus on the accessible service and actual control. A local interface that selects assets, routes trades, controls fees or can suspend access may carry responsibilities even if it cannot alter the base protocol. An immutable protocol may still depend on an oracle, stablecoin, bridge, hosting layer or governance process. Conversely, writing code, holding a token or casting one vote should not be treated as conclusive proof of regulated control, partnership, agency or breach.
292
NIST’s 2026 concept paper on software and AI-agent identity and authorisation is expressly a draft project concept, not a legal standard, but it identifies a concrete design issue: autonomous systems need distinguishable identities and bounded access to data, tools and applications.73 Pakistan should test agent credentials, least privilege, separation of customer accounts, revocation, transaction signing, human confirmation for defined actions and attribution logs before an agent receives custody or execution authority.
293
Early indicators. Relevant signals include material customer losses involving automated execution; growth in agent-controlled wallet permissions; common model, oracle, bridge or cloud dependencies; repeated overrides; actions outside mandate; unexplained correlated trading; prompt-injection or data-poisoning events; DAO administrator-key use; governance concentration; interface delisting or shutdown; and inability to reconstruct why and by whose authority a transaction occurred.
294
Residual risk and implication. Adaptive output cannot always be predicted or explained at transaction-level precision, and a foreign protocol may lack a reachable operator. The design implication is not a fictional electronic person. It is bounded authority, attributable deployment, observable control, intervention capacity, records and loss allocation. High-impact autonomous custody, execution or payment should remain restricted until those elements pass testing. Uncertainty: the capabilities, deployment patterns and genuine autonomy of future agents and protocols are unknown.

26.17.9 Cyber, fraud or institutional-failure shock

295
Assumption. A material event affects one or more critical providers or public institutions: compromise of signing keys; insider theft; corrupted entitlement data; cloud or telecommunications outage; bridge or oracle exploit; ransomware; coordinated fraud; reserve freeze; or absence of a lawfully authorised decision-maker during the event. The scenario is compound because digital-asset failures rarely respect institutional boundaries.
296
The first minutes produce an authority problem. Who can halt withdrawals, isolate a wallet, direct preservation, notify customers, obtain a court order, contact a foreign provider or decide that continued operation is unsafe? A technically capable person without lawful power may protect assets but contaminate evidence or infringe rights; a legally authorised official without keys, data or a working communication channel may be unable to act. Chapter 15’s authentication and responsibility protocol is therefore part of cyber resilience, not separate governance work.
297
The next phase produces a property and evidence problem. The provider must distinguish customer and house assets, valid and fraudulent instructions, on-chain and off-chain liabilities, frozen and lost assets, and recoverable and irrecoverable keys. Investigators need a preserved chain connecting system logs, wallet control, transaction data, device evidence and human decisions. Customers need accurate notice and access to funds that are not lawfully restricted. A premature public attribution can trigger withdrawals or prejudice proceedings; silence can deepen harm.
298
Fraud may exploit the same event. FATF’s 2025 update describes increasing fraud and scam exposure, major theft, stablecoin misuse and low recovery in a significant cross-border case.74 These global findings do not measure Pakistan. They demonstrate why the plan must cover impersonation, false recovery services, compromised customer communications, mule accounts, dormant accounts and rapid layering after the original breach.
299
The the corrective programme package supplies four linked safeguards. First, the customer-asset and failure plan allows reconstruction and transfer. Secondly, the incident standard supplies severity, contacts, update duties and recovery evidence. Thirdly, the investigation SOP supplies preservation, key custody, legal process and return. Fourthly, the Tribunal and court route supplies interim relief and challenge. None is sufficient alone. A technically successful freeze without review can become unlawful deprivation; a perfect evidential chain without customer-asset records may prove the loss but not restore the asset.
300
The exercise should run without advance notice to the operational team while protecting live systems. It should simulate unavailable senior officers, a conflicted service provider, cross-border data, a disputed customer claim and an incorrect initial indicator. The after-action record should distinguish decision, authority, evidence, elapsed time, customer impact, failed dependency and remediation. Security-sensitive details can be protected, but the public should receive an accountable summary.

26.17.10 Cross-border fragmentation or harmonisation

304
Assumption. Foreign jurisdictions continue to implement crypto-asset, stablecoin, tax, payment, sanctions, data and AI rules at different speeds. Some requirements converge through international standards while market-access, custody, issuer, privacy and enforcement rules remain incompatible. Pakistan may face both demands for recognition and restrictions by foreign providers.
305
The scenario stresses section 4 extraterritorial reach and sections 9 and 17 cooperation and recognition powers. An offshore platform may target Pakistan without a conventional establishment; a foreign issuer may serve local wallets but deny local jurisdiction; or a Pakistan licensee may depend on a custodian whose home regulator restricts service. Two authorities may demand inconsistent retention, disclosure, freezing or customer treatment. A memorandum can facilitate exchange but cannot create coercive power or extinguish a Pakistani remedy.
306
The FSB’s 2025 review identifies gaps and inconsistencies in implementation and the resulting opportunity for regulatory arbitrage.75 FATF’s revised Recommendation 16 increases convergence around payment-message information, but international convergence does not itself decide Pakistan’s domestic authority, privacy safeguards or technical interface.76 Tax reporting adds another lane: CARF-style exchange requires domestic fiscal authority, due diligence, secure systems and an international legal basis, not a PVARA licence alone.
307
the corrective programme’s issue-specific recognition model is robust if every reliance decision identifies the activity, comparable outcome, home supervisor, data and record access, customer-asset location, local service and complaint route, enforcement cooperation, transition and withdrawal. Recognition should reduce duplicate evidence only where cooperation works. Material retail, custody, payment or stablecoin exposure may require local conditions even when a foreign licence is respected for a limited function.
308
The contrary path—fragmentation—requires an exit playbook. PVARA should be able to identify affected customers, assets, dependencies and contractual rights; require notice and transfer; preserve records; coordinate with SBP, FMU, tax and enforcement bodies; and state what Pakistan can and cannot compel. An untested promise of international cooperation is not a safeguard.
309
Early indicators. The dashboard should track foreign legal changes affecting Pakistan access; rejected supervisory requests; delayed or incomplete Travel Rule messages; foreign licence restrictions; concentration in jurisdictions without effective cooperation; conflicts over data, sanctions or freezes; sudden terms-of-service changes; blocked redemptions or withdrawals; and growing targeted solicitation by unregistered offshore providers.
310
Residual risk and implication. Pakistan cannot harmonise foreign law unilaterally and should not trade away constitutional, monetary, tax or customer-protection requirements for nominal access. The design implication is selective interoperability with an executable withdrawal route. Uncertainty: foreign reforms, equivalence decisions, geopolitical conditions and provider cooperation are external variables; neither harmonisation nor fragmentation is presumed dominant.

26.17.11 Mining, energy and environmental pathway

311
The detailed legal treatment of mining, electricity connection, tariff, curtailment, metering, grid effects, environmental cost and public-resource allocation belongs with the technical and fiscal analysis in Chapter 4 § 4.7 and Chapter 13. For anticipatory purposes, Chapter 26 retains the governing test: no claim of surplus power or public benefit should be accepted without temporal grid evidence, lawful allocation of public resources, fiscal transparency, environmental measurement, reversible permission and a reasoned decision by the competent authorities. The scenario programme should monitor load concentration, curtailment stress, subsidy exposure, emissions, water use, local distribution and the opportunity cost of alternative public uses.

26.17.12 Rights and distribution under the scenarios

312
None of the scenarios is distributionally neutral. The same control can protect one interest and burden another. Enhanced traceability may reduce fraud but expose financial associations; a rapid freeze may preserve assets but restrict innocent property; high capital requirements may protect customers but concentrate the market; local-presence rules may improve remedy but exclude a useful foreign service; and mining revenue may benefit one region while electricity or environmental cost falls elsewhere.
313
The constitutional frame remains legality, fair treatment, privacy and dignity, economic freedom subject to law, property, equality, information, federal distribution of authority and effective judicial review. These rights do not decide every policy outcome, but they require lawful source, rational classification, proportionality where rights are restricted, reasons and remedy.78

The dashboard should show source, data quality, version and the reason an alert was opened or closed.

26.18 Public Outcomes, Evidence and Accountability

26.18.1 Access, integrity, stability, innovation and rights

322
Success is not the number of licences or transaction volume. Relevant outcomes include safe access, market integrity, customer-asset recovery, payment resilience, lawful innovation, tax compliance, financial inclusion and protected rights. Trade-offs should be reported rather than hidden in a composite score.

26.18.2 Metrics, independent review and legal learning

323
PVARA should publish time-to-decision, refusal and appeal outcomes, complaints, asset shortfalls, incidents, enforcement disposition and market concentration with necessary confidentiality. Independent review should test both legality and effect. Lessons should lead to reasons, consultation and instrument change—not silent modification of forms or software.

26.18.3 Digital Nation finance-sector implementation plan and constitutional tests

324
The responsible federal Division should prepare a digital-assets sectoral implementation plan for Commission approval and Masterplan alignment. The plan should identify statutory outcomes, reform modules, owners, dependencies, resources, procurement, common infrastructure, indicators, risks, public reporting and review. PVARA, SBP, SECP, FMU, FBR, Law Division, relevant investigation bodies, provinces and the PDA should contribute within their mandates.
325
Each proposed national initiative should pass the twelve constitutional planning tests developed in Chapter 6: source of authority; lawful institution; federal–provincial competence; rights; public finance; procurement; data; cyber; regulatory perimeter; remedies; evidence; and revision. A label such as “national wallet”, “tokenised government asset”, “strategic reserve” or “mining hub” supplies none of those answers.

26.18.4 Separating regulatory foundations from market initiatives

326
The plan should contain two portfolios. The regulatory-foundation portfolio covers the amendment Bill, Rules, final Regulations, appointments, Tribunal, registers, interfaces, standards, capacity and tests. The market and public-use portfolio covers sandbox projects, tokenisation, payments, mining, investment promotion or public-sector uses. No initiative in the second portfolio should pass its scale gate until the applicable first-portfolio dependencies are complete.
327
For public-sector tokenisation or wallet use, the sponsoring institution must identify the legal asset, public-money flow, custodian, procurement, accounting, audit, cybersecurity, data, customer or citizen remedy, failure allocation and exit. A pilot should cap exposure and preserve an off-chain authoritative record where law requires. The State should not use a sandbox to avoid fiscal, procurement or constitutional control.

26.18.5 Aligning indicators with public outcomes

328
National reporting should distinguish inputs, outputs, operational outcomes and public outcomes. Staff hired and regulations issued are inputs and outputs. Accurate status, secure customer assets, reduced unresolved complaints, effective detection, predictable decisions, service recovery and lawful tax reporting are operational outcomes. Financial inclusion, investment quality, innovation, lower cross-border friction and reduced illicit finance are public outcomes that require evidence and counterfactual care.
329
The plan should publish baselines, definitions, source systems, frequency, owner and limitations. Commercial market estimates and chain-analytics outputs should be labelled as estimates. Regulatory data should not be repurposed for publicity without legal basis and quality review. The Commission’s monitoring record should disclose delayed dependencies and corrective decisions rather than convert every indicator into a success narrative.

26.19 The Final Proposition

26.19.1 A digital-asset order worthy of code, law and capital

334
Code can represent transactions and assist compliance; law allocates authority, rights and remedies; capital supplies productive risk-bearing. A credible order holds all three together. It allows innovation only through valid institutions and protects citizens without treating technological novelty as suspicion or immunity.

26.19.2 Authority remains human, public and legally accountable

335
The final safeguard is institutional responsibility. Legislatures enact; authorised regulators decide; courts adjudicate; firms remain accountable for their systems; citizens receive reasons and remedies. No dashboard, model, outsourced service or protocol should displace that chain. Pakistan's future order should become more intelligible through technology and remain governed by law.

26.19.3 Bottom line and eight governing design principles

336
Pakistan does not need another declaration that digital assets matter. It needs a controlled conversion of enacted law into an operable legal order. The Virtual Assets Act 2026 (VAA) has already supplied a federal regulator, a functional service perimeter, licensing and issuer modules, customer-asset duties, preventive and supervisory powers, offences, an appellate design and delegated-law powers. The preceding chapters nevertheless show that enactment and operability are not the same thing. A statute can be in force while the entry route is transitional, the final rulebook remains incomplete, a cross-regulator boundary is unresolved, an institution has not been authenticated, or a remedy cannot yet be exercised predictably.30
337
The corrective package therefore begins from law already enacted. It does not treat the field as a clean slate, reproduce the Act in subordinate legislation or replace specialised regulators with a digital-asset super-regulator. It also does not ask regulated businesses to cure defects that belong to the State. A VASP can file a complete application, segregate customer assets and implement controls. It cannot appoint the Authority’s Board, establish a Tribunal, correct a statutory cross-reference, create a tax charge, allocate federal and provincial competence, or convert draft regulations into law. Those tasks must remain with the institutions legally empowered to perform them.

A technical standard cannot impose a penalty not authorised by the Act. Conversely, Parliament need not hard-code every cryptographic control or data field. The reform succeeds when each question is placed at the lowest level that can lawfully and durably answer it, while every higher-order safeguard remains intact.31

Chapter 26 Conclusion and Standalone Contribution

340
Pakistan's 2026 Act and notified Regulations are a genuine legal foundation, but the public task is unfinished. Construction now means making transition precise, completing primary law, issuing final permissions, protecting customer assets, resolving institutional interfaces and testing outcomes. The sequence is legality, capability, operation, review and measured scale. It neither promises an already functioning regime nor understates the authority that valid instruments presently carry.
