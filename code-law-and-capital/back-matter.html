Glossary

1
Definitions state how terms are used in the Treatise. They do not enlarge a statutory definition or create a legal conclusion unsupported by the body and authorities.

adaptive legality

2
The capacity of a legal order to revise rules and institutional responses through lawful, reviewable change without surrendering authority to technical systems. Principal treatment: §§ 1.6, 26.17-26.18.

address

3
A public identifier used to receive or reference digital assets on a network; it is not by itself proof of legal identity, ownership or beneficial entitlement. Principal treatment: §§ 4.3, 14.8.

airdrop

4
A distribution of tokens to addresses or users, whether for promotion, participation, governance or another purpose; its legal and tax treatment depends on function and facts. Principal treatment: §§ 4.4, 13.9.

algorithmic token

5
A token whose supply, price relationship or liquidation behaviour is materially governed by programmed mechanisms rather than solely by a conventional issuer promise. Principal treatment: § 15.7.

anti-money laundering and countering the financing of terrorism (AML/CFT)

6
The legal and institutional system for preventing, detecting, reporting and enforcing against money laundering and terrorist financing. Principal treatment: Chapter 12.

anticipatory legal intelligence

7
The structured examination of plausible future technical, market and institutional developments so that lawful safeguards can be designed before harm materialises. Principal treatment: §§ 1.7, 26.17.

asset-referenced token

8
A token designed to maintain value by reference to one or more assets, rights or indices; the label does not determine its Pakistani legal classification. Principal treatment: §§ 4.4, 19.7.

authority traceability

9
The ability to identify the legal source, decision-maker, delegation, reasons and review route for an exercise of public or private regulatory power. Principal treatment: §§ 1.6, 26.3.

beneficial owner

10
The natural person who ultimately owns or controls a customer, entity or arrangement, or on whose behalf a transaction is conducted, as determined under applicable law. Principal treatment: §§ 12.4, 24.4.

blockchain

11
A form of distributed ledger in which validated records are grouped and linked in an ordered sequence using cryptographic techniques. Principal treatment: §§ 2.2-2.4, 4.2.

bridge

12
A system that transfers or represents value or messages across networks; bridge design can introduce custody, validator, oracle, upgrade and insolvency risks. Principal treatment: §§ 4.6, 11.10.

capital

13
The Treatise's term for value, incentives, market structure, monetary consequences, capability, distribution and the allocation of loss. Principal treatment: Chapter 6.

capital asset

14
Property whose disposal may attract capital-gains treatment under applicable tax law; the classification of a digital asset depends on law, use and facts. Principal treatment: § 13.3.

CARF

15
The OECD Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework for tax-information reporting and exchange; it is not a market licence or a source of domestic legal effect without implementation. Principal treatment: §§ 13.12, 23.8.

central bank digital currency (CBDC)

16
A digital form of central-bank money, distinct from privately issued crypto-assets and stablecoins. Principal treatment: §§ 20.5, 24.12.

classification legibility

17
The ability to explain which legal categories and regulatory perimeters apply to an asset, activity, actor or infrastructure function and why. Principal treatment: §§ 1.6, 5.5.

code

18
The Treatise's term for architecture, execution, protocols, data and the operational facts created by technical systems. Principal treatment: Chapter 4.

cold wallet

19
A wallet arrangement in which signing keys are kept offline or otherwise isolated from ordinary network exposure. Principal treatment: §§ 4.3, 11.6.

consensus

20
The process by which participants in a distributed system agree on the valid ordering or state of records. Principal treatment: §§ 2.2, 4.2.

control

21
Practical or legal power over an asset, system, key, entity or decision. Technical control is evidence, but it is not necessarily ownership or lawful authority. Principal treatment: §§ 4.3, 11.1.

crypto-asset

22
A digital representation of value or rights relying primarily on cryptography and distributed-ledger or similar technology; the term's legal scope varies by instrument. Principal treatment: §§ 2.9, 5.5.

custody

23
The safeguarding or control of assets, keys or entitlements for another person, including the related duties of segregation, reconciliation, security and return. Principal treatment: Chapter 11.

decentralised autonomous organisation (DAO)

24
A participant or governance arrangement coordinated materially through protocol rules, tokens or smart contracts; the name does not resolve entity status or liability. Principal treatment: §§ 2.7, 15.6.

decentralised finance (DeFi)

25
Financial functions delivered through smart contracts and related interfaces, including trading, lending, leverage and liquidity, often with distributed but still identifiable control points. Principal treatment: §§ 2.6, 15.5.

delegated legislation

26
Rules or regulations made under authority conferred by primary legislation and within its scope, procedure and publication requirements. Principal treatment: §§ 5.1, 8.6.

digital asset

27
A digital representation, record, claim or unit capable of carrying value, rights or control; its technical description does not by itself determine legal status. Principal treatment: §§ 1.1, 4.4.

digital identity

28
Data, credentials and processes used to establish or authenticate a person, entity, device or agent in digital interactions. Principal treatment: §§ 7.4, 14.3.

distributed ledger technology (DLT)

29
A system in which an agreed record is replicated or shared across multiple nodes or participants. Principal treatment: §§ 2.2, 4.2.

double-spend problem

30
The risk that the same digital unit is transferred more than once; Bitcoin addressed the problem through distributed validation and proof-of-work. Principal treatment: § 2.3.

electronic money

31
Electronically stored monetary value meeting the definition and regulatory conditions of the applicable payment law; it is not synonymous with every stablecoin or token. Principal treatment: §§ 10.5, 19.7.

enforcement jurisdiction

32
A state's authority and practical ability to compel compliance, seize assets or execute orders, distinct from prescriptive or adjudicative jurisdiction. Principal treatment: § 24.3.

evolutionary legal intelligence

33
The study of how legal and institutional systems inherit, adapt and learn over time, distinguishing genuine institutional development from reactive relabelling. Principal treatment: §§ 1.7, 26.17.

fiat-referenced token

34
A token designed to track the value of a sovereign currency, usually through reserve, redemption or algorithmic arrangements. Principal treatment: §§ 10.9, 26.8.

finality

35
The point at which a transaction or settlement is treated as irreversible or discharged under technical, contractual or legal rules; the three forms may differ. Principal treatment: §§ 4.2, 10.5.

financial integrity

36
The legal and institutional protection of the financial system against laundering, terrorist or proliferation financing, sanctions evasion, fraud and related abuse. Principal treatment: Chapter 12.

fork

37
A divergence in protocol rules or ledger history that can produce competing system states or assets and raise governance, ownership and tax questions. Principal treatment: §§ 4.2, 13.9.

goAML

38
UNODC-supported software used by financial-intelligence units for receiving and analysing reports; use of the platform does not itself determine the underlying legal duty. Principal treatment: § 12.5.

grandfathering

39
An express legal mechanism allowing existing persons or activities to continue under stated conditions after a new regime begins. Principal treatment: §§ 9.7, 26.4.

hash

40
A fixed-length cryptographic output derived from data, used for integrity checks and links between records; it does not prove the truth or legality of the underlying data. Principal treatment: §§ 2.2, 14.8.

human-governed machine-readable regulation

41
A form of structured legal administration in which rules can be represented for computational use while legal authority, reasons, contestability and change remain human and institutional. Principal treatment: § 26.15.

institutional executability

42
The practical capacity of the legally authorised institution to perform its assigned function with resources, systems, records, reasons and review. Principal treatment: §§ 1.6, 16.1.

Intelligent-Order Test

43
The Author's six-part test of classification legibility, authority traceability, institutional executability, rights and remedy, capital intelligibility and adaptive legality. Principal treatment: § 1.6.

issuer

44
A person or arrangement responsible for creating, offering or assuming obligations in relation to a token; the applicable duties depend on the asset and activity. Principal treatment: §§ 9.9, 11.10.

key

45
Cryptographic information used to sign, authorise or verify actions. Possession of a private key may evidence control but does not conclusively establish title or authority. Principal treatment: §§ 4.3, 11.1.

law

46
The Treatise's term for constitutional and legal authority, interpretation, rights, duties, institutions, reasons, evidence and remedy. Principal treatment: Chapter 5.

legal cut-off

47
The date through which current-law claims have been checked for a specified edition; later developments must be handled transparently. Principal treatment: Author's Note; § 1.8.

licence

48
A legally recognised permission to conduct specified regulated activities, subject to scope and conditions; it is distinct from an application, NOC or sandbox admission. Principal treatment: §§ 8.5, 9.8.

market conduct

49
Rules governing fair dealing, disclosure, conflicts, execution, manipulation and treatment of customers in markets. Principal treatment: §§ 11.7-11.9.

mining

50
Participation in transaction validation or block production, commonly through proof-of-work, in return for protocol rewards or fees. Principal treatment: §§ 4.7, 13.9.

monetary sovereignty

51
The state's authority and capacity to govern money, payment, currency and macro-financial conditions within constitutional and international limits. Principal treatment: §§ 6.5, 24.2.

no-objection certificate (NOC)

52
A regulatory instrument indicating no objection within its stated scope and conditions; it is not a full VASP licence unless the controlling law gives it that effect. Principal treatment: §§ 8.10, 9.6.

node

53
A computer or participant that stores, relays, validates or otherwise interacts with a distributed network. Principal treatment: §§ 2.2, 4.2.

non-fungible token (NFT)

54
A token intended to represent a distinct identifier, item, entitlement or record rather than an interchangeable unit; legal effect depends on the underlying rights. Principal treatment: § 4.4.

operative

55
Verified law or instrument in force for the proposition stated. Principal treatment: Author's Note; § 5.4.

oracle

56
A mechanism that supplies external data to a smart contract or protocol and therefore creates a dependency between on-chain execution and off-chain facts. Principal treatment: §§ 4.6, 15.3.

own-account activity

57
Activity undertaken as principal for one's own assets or exposure, distinguished from dealing, brokerage, custody or another service for others. Principal treatment: §§ 9.2-9.3.

peer-to-peer (P2P)

58
A transaction conducted directly between principals or through tools that do not necessarily assume custody or intermediation; repeated conduct may still amount to a regulated service. Principal treatment: §§ 9.3, 10.7.

permissioned ledger

59
A ledger whose participation or validation rights are restricted by identified governance rules or administrators. Principal treatment: § 2.2.

permissionless ledger

60
A ledger designed to allow open participation subject to protocol rules rather than prior institutional admission. Principal treatment: § 2.2.

private international law

61
Rules governing jurisdiction, applicable law and recognition or enforcement in disputes with cross-border private-law elements. Principal treatment: Chapter 25.

proliferation financing

62
The financing of proliferation of weapons of mass destruction and related materials, including obligations arising from targeted financial sanctions. Principal treatment: § 12.6.

proof-of-stake

63
A consensus mechanism in which validation rights and economic exposure are linked to staked assets under protocol rules. Principal treatment: §§ 2.2, 4.7.

proof-of-work

64
A consensus mechanism in which participants expend computational work to propose and validate blocks. Principal treatment: §§ 2.3, 4.7.

protocol

65
The technical rules governing a network or application, including validation, state change, fees, upgrades and participant interaction. Principal treatment: §§ 2.4, 4.2.

public international law

66
International legal rules governing states, international organisations, jurisdiction, responsibility, treaties, sanctions and cooperation. Principal treatment: Chapter 24.

PVARA

67
The Pakistan Virtual Asset Regulatory Authority established by the Virtual Assets Act 2026. Principal treatment: Chapter 8.

regulatory perimeter

68
The boundary determining which authority, law, activity or person falls within a regulatory regime. Principal treatment: §§ 5.5, 10.1.

reserve asset

69
An asset held to support payment, redemption or value-stability obligations; its quality, custody and availability are separate legal and prudential questions. Principal treatment: §§ 6.5, 10.9.

risk-based approach

70
A method of allocating controls and supervision according to assessed risk rather than applying identical measures to all persons and activities. Principal treatment: §§ 12.3, 12.10.

safe harbour

71
An express, bounded protection from specified liability or enforcement when stated conditions are met; it cannot be inferred from silence or administrative tolerance. Principal treatment: §§ 9.7, 26.4.

sandbox

72
A supervised framework for bounded testing under specified conditions; participation does not itself create a general licence or immunity. Principal treatment: §§ 8.6, 15.10.

segregation

73
The legal and operational separation of customer assets from the provider's own assets and, where required, from the assets of other customers. Principal treatment: §§ 11.4, 26.9.

self-hosted wallet

74
A wallet in which the user controls the means of signing rather than relying on a custodial provider; the arrangement does not eliminate all legal duties or evidential questions. Principal treatment: §§ 4.3, 12.7.

settlement

75
The completion of obligations through transfer of money, assets or claims under applicable system, contract and legal rules. Principal treatment: §§ 4.8, 10.5.

smart contract

76
Code that automatically performs or records actions when specified conditions are met; execution does not by itself establish contractual validity or lawful authority. Principal treatment: §§ 2.4, 15.1-15.4.

stablecoin

77
A digital asset designed to maintain a stable value relative to a currency, asset or basket through reserve, redemption, algorithmic or other arrangements. Principal treatment: §§ 2.5, 6.5, 10.9.

strategic legal intelligence

78
The identification of the present legal position, institutional powers, interfaces, risks and lawful options. Principal treatment: §§ 1.7, 26.1-26.4.

suspicious transaction report (STR)

79
A report made to the financial-intelligence authority when legally defined suspicion criteria are met. Principal treatment: § 12.5.

targeted financial sanctions (TFS)

80
Asset-freezing and related prohibitions directed at designated persons, entities or activities under applicable sanctions law. Principal treatment: § 12.6.

token

81
A digitally represented unit, record, claim or governance interest created or managed through a protocol; its legal character depends on rights and functions. Principal treatment: § 4.4.

tokenisation

82
The representation or issuance of an asset, claim, entitlement or governance interest through digital tokens. Principal treatment: §§ 2.5, 6.7, 10.10.

Travel Rule

83
The requirement for specified originator and beneficiary information to accompany or be available in relation to qualifying transfers under applicable AML/CFT rules. Principal treatment: §§ 12.7, 23.2.

validator

84
A participant that verifies or orders transactions or state changes according to a network's consensus rules. Principal treatment: §§ 2.2, 4.7.

virtual asset

85
The term defined by the applicable legal instrument; in the Treatise it must be read according to the relevant statute or standard rather than assumed to equal every digital asset. Principal treatment: §§ 5.5, 8.2.

virtual asset service provider (VASP)

86
A person conducting one or more legally defined virtual-asset services for or on behalf of another person; scope depends on the applicable instrument and actual function. Principal treatment: §§ 4.5, 8.2, 9.1.

wallet

87
Software, hardware or an arrangement used to create, store or use keys and interact with digital assets; a wallet may be custodial, self-hosted or hybrid. Principal treatment: §§ 4.3, 11.3.

wholesale tokenisation

88
Tokenisation designed principally for institutional financial markets, payment, settlement or asset servicing rather than retail circulation. Principal treatment: §§ 20.3, 22.3.
